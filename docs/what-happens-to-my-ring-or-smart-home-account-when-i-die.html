What happens to my Ring or smart-home account when I die?

PARTIAL — Ring sourced directly; Nest not checked

Ring is the one platform in this whole project, so far, that documents a path requiring no death certificate and no court order at all — a genuinely different design than every other vendor in this set. Ring's own support article states: "To take over ownership of a Ring device from someone else, including someone who is deceased or medically incapacitated," the requestor opens the Ring app, taps "Set Up a Device," chooses "Request Ownership," and the request goes to the account currently registered to the device. Source

That's the self-service path when you physically have the device and can't reach the previous owner. The request doesn't transfer instantly — it has to be approved (or lapse), the same way any device transfer works on Ring, and the article doesn't specify a distinct waiting period for the "can't contact the previous owner" scenario. For a more formal request — going through Ring customer support directly rather than the in-app flow — separate, third-party-reported guidance (not independently fetched by us in this pass) indicates Ring asks for "official certified documentation showing you are the authorized person to access the decedent's estate," which we flag below as unverified since we could not locate and fetch that specific support article ourselves.

Nest (and other Google-connected smart-home devices) is a different animal architecturally: Nest accounts run through a Google Account, not a separate Ring-style login. That means Nest almost certainly falls under Google's Inactive Account Manager and deceased-user request process covered in Q1 rather than having its own dedicated death-specific policy — but we did not independently verify that a Nest device specifically inherits IAM's data-sharing behavior (as opposed to just the account login), so treat that as a reasonable inference from Google's account architecture, not a confirmed, sourced fact.

What we could not verify: the specific Ring support article describing the formal (non-app, customer-support-mediated) deceased-owner process — the URL patterns we tried for it (including one a search engine's AI summary described as the "official" article) redirected to the general device-transfer article quoted above, not a dedicated deceased-owner page; and whether Nest's data (not just its device login) is included in what Google's Inactive Account Manager or deceased-user process delivers to a designated contact.

We are CairnVault. We build an encrypted digital-legacy plan — your family can open it when you die, and nobody, including us, can read it while you are alive. So we are not a neutral party on this subject, which is precisely why every claim above links to somebody else's documentation rather than to ours.
