What happens to my Apple/iCloud account when I die?

Apple's Legacy Contact is the most thorough of the big-platform tools, with one major gap. Two things are required to use it, per Apple's support page: "Your Legacy Contact must have both the access key and your death certificate to request access after you pass away." Source

What they get once approved is broad — photos, messages, notes, files, device backups. What they explicitly do not get: "your Legacy Contact can't access certain information. Inaccessible data includes movies, music, books, or subscriptions you purchased with your Apple Account, and data stored in your iCloud Keychain (payment information, passwords, and passkeys)." Passwords are the one category a family settling an estate needs most, and it's the one category walled off. Source

If you never set up a Legacy Contact, Apple still has a fallback: "In the unfortunate event of a customer's death, Apple provides options for their loved ones to request access to or delete their Apple Account." This generally requires "a death certificate, and might also require a court order." In the U.S., a court order naming the requester as the account's rightful inheritor is sufficient on its own — no pre-set access key required for this route, contrary to a claim we previously could not verify and are not repeating. Source (fetched in full, Published Date: April 02, 2026)

One more concrete fact on that same page, directly relevant if you're trying to get into a locked phone: "devices locked with a passcode are protected by passcode encryption, and Apple can't help remove the passcode lock without erasing the device." There is no backdoor — Apple support or a court order can help with the Apple ID/iCloud account, but not with a passcode-locked device itself without wiping it.

What we could not verify: any claim that Apple deletes the account or its data after a fixed number of years post-legacy-access. That language does not appear on Apple's current live page and we are not asserting it.
