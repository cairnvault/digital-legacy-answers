What happens to my Keeper password vault when I die?

FULLY SOURCED

Keeper documents its own emergency-access feature clearly, and it differs from Bitwarden (covered in Q4/Q5) in two concrete, useful ways. Per Keeper's own documentation: "Give trusted family and friends access to your Keeper Vault in the event of an emergency or loss of life. You can designate up to five emergency contacts and decide how much time should pass before their access is granted." Source

Difference one — the feature itself is gated by account type, not by which side of the request you're on: "the Emergency Access feature is only available for consumer accounts." Difference two — Keeper states an actual maximum wait time, something we flagged as unconfirmed for Bitwarden: "The amount of time between your request to provide emergency access and access granted can be set up to three months per contact." That's a hard number Bitwarden's own documentation does not give.

There's a real mechanical difference worth knowing before you rely on this: Keeper requires a pre-existing relationship before emergency access can even be set up. "In order for the recipient to accept the request, you must first establish a 'sharing' relationship if one hasn't already been established through Keeper's record/folder sharing features." In practice, that means naming someone as your emergency contact isn't a single standalone step — you first have to have shared at least one password or file with them the ordinary way, and only after that relationship exists can the emergency designation itself be sent and accepted. Miss that step and the invitation won't go through cleanly.

Once set up, the mechanics match the pattern this whole project keeps finding: there is no death verification anywhere in the flow. A contact requests access, a countdown (up to three months, however you configured it) begins the moment they attempt to log in, and if you don't decline in time, access opens automatically — indistinguishable, from Keeper's side, between you being deceased, incapacitated, or simply not checking your phone.

What we could not verify: what specifically the recipient can see or do once emergency access is granted (full vault takeover vs. read-only, and whether that's configurable per contact) — the documentation page fetched here covers setup and timing mechanics, not the resulting access scope in the same level of detail Bitwarden's own pages provide.
