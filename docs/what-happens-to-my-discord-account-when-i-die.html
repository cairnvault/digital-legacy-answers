What happens to my Discord account when I die?

FULLY SOURCED

Discord's policy is unusually explicit for a platform this size, and its bottom line is blunt: deletion is on the table, access never is. Per Discord's own support article, "Deceased or Incapacitated Users": "Due to privacy and security reasons, we are unable to provide access, disclose personal information, make changes, or share data tied to an account." Source, retrieved via a Wayback Machine snapshot dated 8 December 2025 after Discord's live help pages returned a bot-protection block to every direct fetch attempt in this pass

If the requestor has the login, they can delete the account themselves through Account Removal in Settings — Discord treats that exactly like any other self-service deletion. Without login access, but with access to the email on the account, a simple written statement is enough: "I confirm that I would like to delete the account associated with [emailaddress@email.com]." Requesting from a different email entirely requires real documentation: the deceased's email and username, a "Death certificate or coroner's report," proof of relationship ("Marriage license, birth certificate, last will and testament, estate letter, or power of attorney"), and the requestor's own valid photo ID or passport.

Two details in this article are worth knowing before you need them. First, Discord states outright: "We do not currently have a feature to memorialize or transfer accounts" — unlike Facebook or LinkedIn, there is no in-between option; deletion (or doing nothing) are the only outcomes. Second, Discord runs its own inactivity clock independent of any request: "Discord accounts that have not been used for 2 years or more may be scheduled to be deleted due to inactivity," with a warning email or text sent to the account's contact info as that mark approaches — meaning an untouched Discord account belonging to someone who died will eventually vanish on its own even if no family member ever contacts Discord at all.

What we could not verify: Discord's live help center blocked every direct fetch attempt (both a browser-user-agent curl request and a rendering fetch tool) in this research pass; the quotes above come from a Wayback Machine snapshot roughly eight months old, and we could not confirm the current live page still says the same thing word for word.
